1. Responsible AI
An AI answer gives a convincing quotation and a source title. What is the best next step?

  1. Use it because a source is named.
  2. Find the original source and check that the quotation is really there.
  3. Ask the same AI to say it more confidently.

2. Responsible AI
You want help understanding a household budget. What is the most appropriate input for this classroom task?

  1. A full bank statement with account numbers.
  2. Your friend’s bank statement with only their name removed.
  3. A fictional table of rounded income and expense categories.

3. Responsible AI
An AI says that €200 earning 10% interest once becomes €210. Which response best checks the answer?

  1. Calculate €200 × 1.10 = €220 independently.
  2. Accept it because the arithmetic looks simple.
  3. Copy the answer into a second paragraph.

4. Digital safety
A message asks you to pay a parcel fee through an unfamiliar link. What should you do?

  1. Pay because the fee is small.
  2. Open the carrier’s known app or website independently and check the delivery.
  3. Reply with your card number to confirm the fee.

5. Digital safety
A “support agent” contacts you and asks for your one-time sign-in code. What should you do?

  1. Send it if the agent knows your name.
  2. Send half the code first.
  3. Do not share it; contact the service through a known route.

6. Digital safety
A suspicious message has perfect spelling and the correct logo. What does that establish?

  1. The organisation definitely sent it.
  2. It cannot contain a harmful link.
  3. Very little about its origin; those features can be copied.

7. Financial resilience
A basket costs €100. Its price rises 10%, then another 10%. What is the final cost?

  1. €120
  2. €121
  3. €110

8. Financial resilience
Monthly income is €900, spending is €750 and planned savings are €100. How much is unallocated?

  1. €50
  2. €150
  3. €250

9. Financial resilience
Which statement about diversification is most accurate?

  1. Owning many assets always prevents losses.
  2. It removes every kind of risk.
  3. It can reduce concentration risk, but holdings may still fall together.

After the sessions / Learner form B

Try a fresh situation.

12 minutes9 questionsOne answer each

Use your paper code, not your name. Circle one answer for each question. Work independently; a basic calculator is allowed. Choose the best answer. This is a learning check, not a judgement about your personal finances.

Paper code: __________________   Date: __________________

1. Responsible AI
An AI names a research report to support a surprising claim. What is the best next step?

  1. Repeat the claim because the report sounds official.
  2. Ask the AI to invent a shorter title.
  3. Locate the report and read the passage that should support the claim.

2. Responsible AI
You want an AI tool to help practise a conversation about money. Which input is most appropriate?

  1. An invented scenario with only the details needed for practice.
  2. A private conversation copied from a classmate’s phone.
  3. A photo of your bank card so the example feels realistic.

3. Responsible AI
An AI says a €60 item with a 25% discount costs €50. Which response checks it?

  1. Accept the answer if it is written clearly.
  2. Calculate €60 × 0.75 = €45 independently.
  3. Ask for the same answer in a different font.

4. Digital safety
A message from a new number claims to be your friend and urgently requests money. What should you do?

  1. Verify with the friend through an established contact route.
  2. Send money because the display name is familiar.
  3. Share a sign-in code to prove that you are available.

5. Digital safety
You entered your password on a page reached through a suspicious message. What is the most useful immediate response?

  1. Wait to see whether anything happens.
  2. Open the real service independently, change the password and review account sessions.
  3. Send the password to the sender and ask if the page was real.

6. Digital safety
A website shows a padlock or HTTPS indicator. What can you conclude?

  1. Every claim on the page is true.
  2. The business is definitely legitimate.
  3. The connection is encrypted; that alone does not establish trustworthiness.

7. Financial resilience
A basket costs €200. Its price rises 5%, then another 5%. What is the final cost?

  1. €210
  2. €220
  3. €220.50

8. Financial resilience
Monthly income is €1,100, spending is €900 and planned savings are €120. How much is unallocated?

  1. €80
  2. €200
  3. €320

9. Financial resilience
Two funds mostly own shares in the same industry. Which statement is most accurate?

  1. Two fund names guarantee broad diversification.
  2. Their returns may move together, so concentration can remain.
  3. Holding them eliminates market risk.

Facilitator edition / Scoring + interpretation

Notice the learning.

This is an original classroom assessment, not a validated instrument. Forms A and B cover parallel concepts; their statistical equivalence has not been established. Do not use the scores for certification, diagnosis or claims of proven programme impact.

Pair forms without collecting names

Prepare shuffled cards with unique random codes, such as M7Q4. Give one to each learner; ask them to retain it and copy it onto both forms. Do not derive codes from birthdays, initials or phone numbers, and do not keep a name-to-code list. Participants may decline. If a code is lost, do not guess a match. Keep forms secure and set a retention period before collecting them.

Score consistently

A correct answer earns 1 point; an incorrect, blank or multiply selected answer earns 0. Total: 0–9. Module subscores: responsible AI Q1–3 (0–3), digital safety Q4–6 (0–3), financial resilience Q7–9 (0–3). Record missing answers separately so that a blank is not mistaken for a demonstrated misconception.

For a paired learner, change = Form B score − Form A score (range −9 to +9). Example: 5/9 to 7/9 is +2 points, or +22.2 percentage points of the nine-point scale. It is not evidence of a 22.2% increase in an underlying ability.

Report honestly. Give the number who completed each form and the number with matched codes. Summarise paired scores and the most common misconceptions; keep individual results private. Different item difficulty, practice, attendance and outside learning can affect change. Without a suitable comparison design, an increase cannot by itself establish that the programme caused it.

Use the discussion to decide what to teach again. Scores from three questions per module are too coarse to support a detailed judgement about an individual learner.
