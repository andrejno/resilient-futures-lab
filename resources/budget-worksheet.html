Financial resilience / Worked answer

The numbers tell a story.

A good budget is more than a total that adds up. It shows which decisions are still open.

Before the surprise

Income €1,200
Spending €990
Planned transfers €160
Unallocated €50

After a €120 repair

Use the €50 margin first.
The remaining gap is €70.
Make an explicit adjustment to cover it.

One possible revised plan

ChangeAmount €
Reduce leisure from €90 to €4050 freed
Pause subscriptions from €20 to €020 freed
Spending after cuts, before the repair920
Add the repair120
Revised spending1,040
Keep the original savings and reserve transfers160
Unallocated money: €1,200 − €1,040 − €1600

This plan balances, but leaves no margin for another surprise. It also assumes the subscriptions can be paused immediately without a fee and that €40 for leisure is acceptable. Check those assumptions before calling the plan workable.

A different plan

If Alex already has an emergency fund, the €50 margin plus €70 withdrawn from that fund covers the repair while leaving the original plan intact. The balance of the fund is €70 lower than it would otherwise have been. Alternatively, reducing this month’s emergency-fund transfer from €150 to €80 frees €70; it also slows the fund’s growth. These are different cash flows—do not count the same €70 twice.

Three things to notice

  1. Saving is a transfer. Money moves between Alex’s accounts. It is not consumed, but it is no longer unallocated cash in the current account.
  2. Annual costs still belong in a monthly plan. €120 ÷ 12 = €10 a month. This works only with 12 months to prepare; a bill due next month needs a different plan.
  3. Categories need context. Transport or internet access may be essential for work or education. A small leisure allowance can help make a plan sustainable.
Try a second shock: income falls to €1,050 before the month begins. With the original €1,150 allocation, the shortfall is €100. Suggest a feasible revision. Explain what you cannot infer without knowing more about Alex’s commitments.

Teaching key: accept any correctly calculated, feasible plan with clear assumptions. Do not reward cutting necessary food, health care or housing simply because it makes the arithmetic easy.
